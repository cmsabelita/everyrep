Use Screen Time to make yourself work out

Screen Time limits are one tap from "Ignore". EveryRep ties the lock to your training plan: the apps open when today's workout is logged.

Why plain Screen Time limits don't get you to the gym

A daily limit counts minutes, not workouts. It locks you out on rest days, lets you in on training days the moment you tap "Ignore limit", and knows nothing about the session you planned at 6 pm.

EveryRep uses the same Screen Time system Apple built for this, but the schedule comes from your program. Training days lock, rest days don't, and logging the workout is what opens your apps.

Set it up in a minute

  1. Build your program

    Tell EveryRep your days, equipment and experience during onboarding.

  2. Allow Screen Time

    Tap Allow on Apple's prompt. EveryRep only applies the lock you choose.

  3. Pick apps and a training time

    Choose the apps to lock and when training starts, or lock them all day on training days.

  4. Train, log, unlock

    Finish today's workout and the apps open until tomorrow.

Questions

Can I use Screen Time to make myself work out?

Screen Time on its own can limit apps, but it can't tell whether you trained. EveryRep uses Screen Time to lock the apps you choose on training days and opens them when your workout is logged.

What permission does EveryRep ask for?

Apple's Screen Time permission. Tap Allow, then pick the apps to lock. You can skip this step and add the lock later.

Can I lock apps all day instead of from my training time?

Yes. Turn on "Lock all day until I train" and the apps lock from midnight on training days until the workout is logged.

Does it need an account?

No. There is no account or sign-in, and your app use stays on your iPhone.

Which iPhones does it work on?

iPhones running iOS 17 or later.
